I Want to Learn Video Editing in 2026. Where Should I Start?

If video editing feels confusing from the outside, start smaller. You don't need every effect, a powerful computer, or a perfect first video. You need a clear project and a repeatable way to finish it.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 7-minute read

Maybe you want to make better social videos, create tutorials, help a small business show its products, or simply bring an idea to life. Whatever brought you here, the first step is the same: learn the basics by making something short.

Start with one small video

Choose a project you can finish in 30–60 seconds: a mini tutorial, a short story, a product demonstration, or a simple day-in-the-life clip. A small project lets you practise the whole process instead of getting stuck watching tutorials forever.

Use the phone or computer you already have. Before downloading an app or paying for a course, check that the editor runs on your device and that its free version covers what you need.

Learn these four skills first

  • Cutting and trimming: remove mistakes, long pauses, and anything that distracts from the point.
  • Sequence: arrange clips so the video has a clear beginning, middle, and end.
  • Audio: make speech easy to hear and keep any background music quieter than the voice.
  • Text and captions: use readable captions and short headings, then check automatic captions for errors.

These fundamentals are useful across editing apps. Fancy transitions and advanced colour effects can wait until you have completed a few videos.

Your first-edit workflow

  1. Write down the one thing the video should communicate.
  2. Record a few short clips and choose the clearest takes.
  3. Import them into your editor and make a rough cut.
  4. Put the clips in a logical order and remove unnecessary pauses.
  5. Adjust audio, add captions, and use only the transitions you need.
  6. Export in a format suitable for the platform where you plan to share it.
  7. Watch the exported file from beginning to end, preferably on the device your audience will use.
Beginner rule: finish one simple video before starting five ambitious ones. Each completed edit teaches you more than endlessly collecting tutorials.

How do you choose an editing app?

Pick based on your device, budget, and the kind of videos you want to make. Look for a timeline that's understandable, reliable trimming, text and caption tools, audio controls, and export options that fit your needs. Try a short test project before committing to a paid plan.

Don't choose an app just because a creator says it's the best. The best starting tool is one you can access, understand, and practise with consistently.

Should you learn from tutorials or a structured course?

Free tutorials are useful when you have a specific question, such as how to trim a clip or fix uneven audio. The downside is that random videos can leave gaps in your learning.

A structured beginner course may help if you prefer lessons arranged in a planned sequence. Before buying, read the curriculum, check which software it teaches, confirm the current price, and make sure the format suits your device and learning style.

Your first-week challenge

  1. Day 1: choose an app and learn how its timeline works.
  2. Day 2: practise trimming and arranging five clips.
  3. Day 3: make a short video with a clear beginning and ending.
  4. Day 4: add captions and check them for mistakes.
  5. Day 5: improve the audio and pacing.
  6. Day 6: export and watch the video on your phone.
  7. Day 7: make a second version using one lesson from the first.

You don't have to know everything to begin. Pick a small idea, complete the edit, and improve the next one. That's how the skill grows: one finished video at a time.
